Useful Language

Asking for information

  • Where is the meeting room?
  • What time do we finish on Fridays?
  • Is there a place to leave my bicycle?
  • Could you show me where the kitchen is, please?

Welcoming a new colleague

  • Welcome to the team!
  • Let me show you around.
  • Your desk is next to mine.
  • The break is from one to two.
  • If you need help, just ask.

Choose the question

Choose the question that matches each answer.

1. “It’s next to the kitchen.”
2. “Yes, there’s one at the back of the building.”
3. “We close at six.”

Modal Verbs of Obligation

Workplace rules

  • Must and have to express obligation. Staff must wear a badge. Sarah has to sign in.
  • Mustn’t means something is against the rules. You mustn’t share your password.
  • Don’t have to and doesn’t have to mean something is optional. You don’t have to wear a tie. Sarah doesn’t have to bring lunch.

Rules or choices?

Choose the form that fits each workplace situation.

1. Visitors can enter only after they sign in. They ___ sign in.
2. There’s no rule about ties. You ___ wear one.
3. Only managers can use this door. New employees ___ use it.
4. Every employee needs to attend the safety briefing. You ___ go.
5. Lunch is provided. Sarah ___ bring her own.
6. The dress code says no shorts. Staff ___ wear shorts.

Choose the correct form

Check the word order and the verb form.

1. Complete the sentence with a form of “have to”. Sarah ___ wear a name badge.
2. Choose the correct question.
3. Choose the correct sentence.
4. Choose the correct instruction.

Write Your Questions

Five questions for your manager

Imagine it’s your first day at a new job. Write five questions to ask your manager.

Try starting with Where…?, What time…?, Is there…? or Could you…?

Show example questions

  2. Where can I park my car?
  3. Is there a staff room?
  4. What should I wear to work?
